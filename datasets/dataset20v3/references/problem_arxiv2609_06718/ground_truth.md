# Reference experiments — problem arxiv2609_06718

**Claim.** A single deployable humanoid soccer control policy driven by high-level skill commands can jointly execute heterogeneous ball-interaction skills, transition between them, and complete long-horizon compositions after simulation-to-real transfer. The claim is supported only if, under an equal training budget, the policy’s full-sequence success rate is strictly higher than that of the strongest prior single-stage multi-skill controller at both evaluated composition horizons, and its physical-robot success rate is strictly higher than a matched conventional motion-prior controller on every evaluated atomic and composite task. Full-sequence success requires completing every commanded subskill in order rather than merely reaching an intermediate stage.

### Experiment 0

Establish that the unified policy can execute the three atomic soccer skills before testing compositions. Train Vanilla PPO, AMP, Conditional AMP, MoE-based AMP, and SkillX in NVIDIA Isaac Lab on the same 25-DoF Noetix E1 model, simulation settings, observation space, task rewards, reference-motion dataset, evaluation protocol, and shared PPO hyperparameters, then evaluate each method over 100 trials per atomic task with domain randomization and observation corruption disabled. For trapping, initialize an incoming ball 1.0–2.0 m away at 0.5–1.5 m/s and count success when its planar speed falls below 0.3 m/s within 20 s. For dribbling, initialize the ball 0.5–1.0 m away and count success when it reaches within 0.5 m of a target sampled at x ∈ [4.5, 5.5] m and y ∈ [−2.0, 2.0] m within 20 s. For shooting, initialize the robot 4–6 m from the goal and the stationary ball 0.5–1.2 m away, and count success when the ball crosses the goal line within 15 s. Vanilla PPO obtains 9.0% shooting, 97.0% trapping in 3.90 s, and 5.0% dribbling in 7.26 s; AMP obtains 87.0%, 99.0% in 5.22 s, and 94.0% in 6.27 s; Conditional AMP obtains 1.0%, 100.0% in 5.84 s, and 8.0% in 6.70 s; MoE-based AMP obtains 1.0%, 99.0% in 5.77 s, and 36.0% in 5.90 s; SkillX obtains 95.0%, 100.0% in 5.31 s, and 100.0% in 5.62 s, respectively.

### Experiment 1

Test full command-driven skill composition under the shared simulation training budget. Compare Vanilla PPO, AMP, Conditional AMP, MoE-based AMP, MoE-Encoder AMP, and SkillX using the same task rewards, maximum training budget, randomized initial conditions, and evaluation protocol, with 1000 sequential single-environment trials per method and event-triggered command changes. The Medium task requires DRIBBLE→SHOOT: the robot starts near midfield, dribbles a ball initialized 0.6–1.2 m away to the penalty-point waypoint, switches to SHOOT, and succeeds only if the ball enters the goal within 30 s. SkillX records 100.0% conditional dribbling success, 88.0% conditional shooting success, 88.0% full-task success, and 12.0 s mean completion time over successful episodes. The corresponding overall success rates are 0.0% for Vanilla PPO, 51.0% for AMP, 11.2% for Conditional AMP, 42.6% for MoE-based AMP, and 67.2% for MoE-Encoder AMP; their completion times where defined are 12.6, 17.6, 16.6, and 13.6 s. The Hard task requires TRAP→DRIBBLE×3→SHOOT: an incoming ball begins at 0.5–1.0 m/s, the command switches from TRAP when ball speed falls below 0.3 m/s, the robot dribbles through waypoints (−6.25, 1.145), (0, −1.145), and (9.75, 0), and then switches to SHOOT; success requires the ball to enter the goal within 40 s. SkillX records 100.0% trapping, 100.0% conditional dribbling, 81.7% conditional shooting, 81.7% full-task success, and 28.4 s completion time. Overall Hard-task success is 0.0% for Vanilla PPO, 27.4% for AMP, 0.0% for Conditional AMP, 5.0% for MoE-based AMP, and 45.6% for MoE-Encoder AMP; AMP, MoE-based AMP, and MoE-Encoder AMP complete successful episodes in 30.4, 32.6, and 34.7 s, respectively. Substage rates are conditional on reaching that stage, whereas overall success requires completing every stage in order.

### Experiment 2

Isolate which SkillX components affect full-sequence composition. Evaluate the Full Model and controlled simulation ablations on the Medium and Hard tasks using overall success rate. The Full Model obtains 88.0% and 81.7%. Replacing the skill-specific discriminators with one shared discriminator gives 79.5% and 79.3%; replacing skill-specific value heads with one shared critic gives 82.5% and 65.0%; flattening the same five-frame history directly into the actor gives 49.0% and 20.0%; replacing the Transformer with an MLP while retaining the auxiliary objectives gives 61.9% and 47.6%; removing only ball-velocity estimation gives 83.6% and 44.4%; removing all velocity-estimation objectives gives 79.7% and 15.8%; using a history MLP without auxiliary objectives gives 78.0% and 32.6%; and sampling the final command-duration range from the start instead of progressively shortening command segments gives 85.5% and 30.4%.

### Experiment 3

Check whether the component ablations also alter the atomic skills used in the compositions. Evaluate SkillX, the shared-discriminator variant, the shared-critic variant, and Raw Stacked History over 100 trials on shooting, trapping, and dribbling. SkillX obtains 95.0% shooting success, 100.0% trapping success in 5.31 s, and 100.0% dribbling success in 5.62 s. Removing skill-specific AMP gives 87.0%, 100.0% in 2.77 s, and 100.0% in 5.60 s; removing the skill-specific critic gives 85.0%, 100.0% in 5.32 s, and 100.0% in 6.66 s; Raw Stacked History gives 16.0%, 98.0% in 5.77 s, and 91.0% in 10.80 s.

### Experiment 4

Retest trapping with a stricter post-contact success definition. Define first robot–ball contact as planar distance at most 0.4 m, and count a trial as successful only if, within 3 s after contact, the ball stays below 0.25 m/s and remains within 0.5 m of the robot for at least 0.5 s. Over 100 trials per method, SkillX succeeds in 63.0%, AMP in 31.0%, Conditional AMP in 24.0%, and MoE-based AMP in 50.0%.

### Experiment 5

Test the learned controller on the physical Noetix E1 with the same sensing and deployment interface used by the AMP baseline. Run SkillX and standard AMP for 10 hardware trials each on four MoCap-based tasks: dribbling to a target region, shooting to the goal, two-step dribbling with a direction change, and dribble-then-shoot. Composite-task commands are manually triggered when the current subtask is completed. A trial succeeds only if the robot completes the objective without falling and the ball remains inside the valid field boundary; composite success requires every subtask in the specified order. AMP succeeds in 1/10 dribbling trials, 1/10 shooting trials, 0/10 two-step-dribbling trials, and 0/10 dribble-then-shoot trials. SkillX succeeds in 8/10, 8/10, 7/10, and 6/10 trials, respectively.

### Experiment 6

Test whether the deployment interface can operate without external motion capture. Deploy SkillX on the physical Noetix E1 using a head-mounted ZED2i camera, visual-inertial odometry, and YOLOv8 ball detection running at approximately 30 Hz; convert the estimated robot, ball, and fixed-target states into the same robot-centric interface used in simulation and MoCap deployment. Train for this deployment with lower-rate object updates, noise, random delays, frozen observations when the ball is outside the simulated camera field of view, and camera range and field-of-view constraints. The authors run the dribble-then-shoot composite behavior and report that the robot localizes the ball, approaches it, and executes closed-loop ball interaction without MoCap, but they provide no trial count or success rate for this deployment.

### Experiment 7

Test the framework on commanded whole-body skills beyond ball interaction. Remove external-object observations and object-interaction rewards, retain the SkillX training framework, and train one proprioception- and command-conditioned policy on kicking, saluting, back kicking, single-leg standing, left and right defending motions, and run-to-shoot. The authors report that the single policy executes these skills and transitions among them, but provide no quantitative success rate or transition metric.
